02 — Research

Radiation-free 3D surface topography for scoliosis screening and trunk asymmetry assessment.

My MSc thesis develops 3D surface topography methods for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) in two parts: an interpretable measure of trunk asymmetry, and a deep learning model for screening. Both start from the same idea: comparing each torso surface with its mirrored copy.

A surface-topography measure of trunk asymmetry

Developed a global and regional 3D surface-topography measure of external trunk asymmetry, related it to established clinical assessments, and made it interpretable for clinicians with reference percentiles.

Goal
A radiation-free measure of external trunk shape that complements, rather than replaces, clinical and radiographic assessment.
Measure
Each torso is compared with its mirrored, rigidly aligned copy. The deviations are summarized as RMSE over the whole torso and over overlapping thoracic, thoracolumbar, and lumbar regions.
Clinical link
Compared with trunk rotation, rib hump, Cobb angle, and the TRACE aesthetic score in an independent clinical cohort.
Interpretation
Gaussian mixture models express each RMSE value as a percentile relative to healthy and AIS reference groups.

Presented at

  • Apr 2026SOSORT, Turin Poster

Deep learning model for AIS screening

Designed a four-map, dual-pathway convolutional neural network that distinguishes adolescents with AIS from those with typically developing spines, and evaluated it against a previously developed posterior-only model on the same participant-separated test set.

Goal
Reduce unnecessary X-ray referrals: high specificity while keeping sensitivity high.
Methods
Separate deviation and depth pathways combine four maps from the front (anterior) and back (posterior) of the torso. Scans from three sites were split by participant into training, validation, and test sets.
Impact
A promising radiation-free candidate aid for AIS screening.
